\raggedleft\section{INTRODUCTION}
\end{flushright}
\hrulefill

\newpage
\justifying

Fetal growth restriction is, despite advances in neonatal care and uptake of antenatal ultrasound scanning, still a mojor cause of perinatal morbidity. Neonates with birth weight \textgreater10th percentile are assumed to be Appropriate-for-Gestational Age(AGA), alothough many are at increased risk of perinatal morbidity, because of undetected mild restriction of growth potential.

One of the most important risk factors for stillbirth is fetal growth restriction. In many cases, fetal growth restriction reflects placental insufficiency, where the placenta is functioning sub-optimally in its role to supply oxygen and nutrients, the fetus fails to maintain adequate growth in utero, and is unable to reach its biological growth potential. While being small-for-gestational-age due to placental insufficiency is major risk factor for still birth, stillbirths also occur in appropriate for gestaional age(AGE \textgreater10th centile) fetuses. AGA fetuses are plausibly also at risk of still birth if placental insufficiency is present. Such fetuses may be expected to demonstrate declining growth trajectory across pregnancy, although they do not fall below the 10th centile before birth.

\end{flushright}
\hrulefill
\newpage

\textbf{PRIMARY OBJECTIVE}:

\begin{itemize}
    \item To determine if reduced growth velocity in AGA fetuses is linked to placental insufficiency indicators and whether these fetuses might be at an increased risk of adverse outcomes similar to SGA fetuses.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{SECONDARY OBJECTIVE:}

\begin{itemize}
    \item To analyse fetal outcomes of AGA fetuses with reduced growth velocity
\end{itemize}

\begin{flushright}
    \section{JUSTIFICATION FOR THE STUDY}
\end{flushright}
\hrulefill
\newpage

By intensely monitoring the growth velocity of the AGA fetuses antenatally the effects and risks of placental insufficiency like still birth could be reduced and can be managed with planned delivery at term. The research emphasizes the need to consider growth velocity as a crucial parameter in prenatal care especially for fetuses not traditionally categorized as at risk due to their birth weight percentile.

    \section{MATERIALS AND METHODS}
\end{flushright}
\hrulefill
\newpage

\textbf{\large SELECTION OF CASES:} 

This is a prospective observational study conducted at the Department of Obstetrics, Government Raja Mirasudhar Hospital attaced to Thanjavur Medical College from September 2024 to August 2025. All antenatal women who are admitted with reduced growth velocity in the third trimester who fulfill the selection criteria will be included in the study. Patient identity will be kept confidential. Access to records for persons other than researchers and guide will be prohibited.

\textbf{\large INCLUSION CRITERIA}
\begin{itemize}
    \item All antenatal women presenting with reduced growth velocity either admitted in the ward or presenting in general OPD or casualty
    \item Singleton pregnancy
    \item Mothers who consent to being included in the study
\end{itemize}

\textbf{\large EXCLUSION CRITERIA}
\begin{itemize}
    \item fetal infection
    \item low-lying placenta
    \item hypertension
    \item estimated fetal weight below the 10th centile at the first ultrasound
    \item diagnosed IUFD
    \item congenital malformation
    \item women with multiple pregnancies
    \item antepartum hemorrhage 
    \item mothers who refuse to give consent 
\end{itemize}

\textbf{\large SAMPLE SIZE:}

Based on previous studies in out institution, approximately 7\% of fetuses experienced third trimester growth velocity lower than 30centiles who were Appropriate-for-Gestational age. With 80\% power and $\alpha$=0.05 and entering these values in openepi, a sample size of 101 is required. 

\textbf{\large STATISTICAL METHODS:}

This prospective observational study was carried out on pregnant women either outpatient or inpatient with reduced growth velocity at third trimester at Government Rajah Mirasudhar Hospital for a period of 12 months. A detailed history of the pregnancy women included in the study is taken. Thorough clinical examination including the patient's vital signs, systemic and obstetric examination were carried out on admission. All preliminary investigations including ultrasound were done. The patients were followed up regularly in antenatal aclinic and repeat ultrasound scans were done as required.

A structed proforma was used to collect all the information and was followed till their pregnancy termination. Ultrasound assessments were done at 28 and 36 weeks of gestation to measure the estimated fetal weight and abdominal circumference. EFW and AC is correlated with clinical indicators of placental insufficiency. All patients were followed throughout the antepartum, intrapartum and 3 months postpartum. Post partum followup was performed through telephone. All data was entered and analysed using Microsoft excel and descriptive analyses was done.
